2. How to chat safely

Chatting with AI is like talking to a helpful stranger. Most of the conversation is fine, but there are a few things you should never say.

The golden rule

Never share private or sensitive information with an AI. Once you type it, you cannot take it back.

Never share these

Do not type any of the following into a free AI chat:

  • Your full name, address, phone number, or email
  • Passwords, PINs, or security codes
  • Credit card numbers or bank account numbers
  • Social Security number
  • Medical records, diagnoses, or prescription details
  • Someone else’s private information (family, friends, patients, clients)
  • Work secrets, confidential documents, or your employer’s private data

Why

Free AI chats may be saved, reviewed, or used to improve the AI. You do not control who sees what you type. Assume anything you type could become public.

Safe habits

  1. Use nicknames, not real names. Say “my boss” instead of your boss’s real name. Say “my clinic” instead of its real name.
  2. Remove details before pasting. If you paste an email or letter, delete names, addresses, phone numbers, and account numbers first.
  3. Ask general questions instead. Instead of “What is wrong with me based on these symptoms?” ask “What are common causes of a headache?” instead.
  4. Log out when you are done, especially on a shared or public computer.
  5. Check your employer’s rules before using AI at work. Some workplaces forbid it entirely.

If you already shared something by mistake

Do not panic. You cannot delete it from the AI’s memory, but you can:

  • Stop sharing more information in that chat
  • Start a new chat for future conversations
  • Change any passwords you may have typed
  • Contact your IT department if it was work information

Healthcare workers

AI can help with administrative tasks like drafting shift notes (from templates without patient names) or explaining general health topics in plain language. Never put real patient information into a free AI chat. That would violate privacy laws (such as HIPAA in the United States) and your employer’s policies. AI is never a substitute for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
