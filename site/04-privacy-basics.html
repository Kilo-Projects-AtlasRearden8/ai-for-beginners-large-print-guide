4. Privacy basics

Privacy means keeping your personal information to yourself. When you use free AI, you are using someone else’s computer. That means what you type may be stored, read by people, or used to train future AI.

The short version

Assume anything you type into a free AI chat could be seen by other people. Do not type anything you would not want posted on a public bulletin board.

What to protect

Keep these out of AI chats:

  • Names (yours, family, friends, patients, clients, coworkers)
  • Addresses and phone numbers
  • Email addresses
  • Passwords and security codes
  • Bank account and credit card numbers
  • Social Security numbers
  • Medical information about real people
  • Your employer’s private or confidential information

Before you paste anything

If you want to paste an email, letter, or document into an AI:

  1. Read through it first.
  2. Replace real names with words like “my boss,” “my patient,” or “my client.”
  3. Remove addresses, phone numbers, account numbers, and dates of birth.
  4. Only paste what you need for the task.

Browser and account tips

  • Sign out of the AI when you are done, especially on a shared computer.
  • Use a private or “incognito” browser window if you are on a shared device.
  • Do not save passwords in the browser on a shared computer.
  • Clear your browser history if you are using a public or library computer.

Healthcare workers and patient privacy

If you work in healthcare, patient information is protected by law (in the United States, this is HIPAA). Putting real patient information — even just a first name and a diagnosis — into a free AI chat can break the law and your employer’s rules.

Safe use for healthcare workers:

  • Use AI for general education: “What are common signs of dehydration in older adults?”
  • Use AI for admin drafting with templates that contain no patient data.
  • Never paste real patient charts, notes, or identifiers into a free AI chat.
  • Follow your employer’s AI policy at work.

Is anything truly private?

Free AI tools usually are not private. If you need true privacy, look for tools that explicitly say they do not store or train on your data, or run AI locally on your own computer. This guide focuses on free tools, which you should treat as public.

Checklist before you type

  • I am not sharing my name, address, phone, or email.
  • I am not sharing passwords or account numbers.
  • I am not sharing anyone else’s private information.
  • I am not sharing real patient or client details.
  • I have removed names and identifying details from anything I pasted.

If you checked every box, you are probably safe to proceed.
